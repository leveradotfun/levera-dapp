LYC is a dollar claim and [vLYC](/protocol/leverage#vlyc-the-virtual-senior-unit) is denominated
in dollars, so a claim against a BTC pool and one against an ETH pool are **the same unit**. The
senior token does not care which asset backs it.

The risk machinery does. Each collateral carries its own oracle, its own venue for cash conversions,
its own collateral ratio, its own cap on how much of the book it may back, and its own price for
renting senior against it.

<Note>
Adding this required **no change to `Launch`**. Every path that mints or burns senior already routes
through `lendIdle`, `moveSeniorToIdle`, `accrueFunding` or `receiveSeniorInKind`, and every path that
moves collateral through `addPoolEth` / `subPoolEth` — all `onlyPool`, so the asset is attributable
from `msg.sender` alone. That mattered: `Launch` spends its entire budget against the EIP-170 cap.
</Note>

## The quote asset is the collateral

A launch is **denominated in** its collateral, **pairs against** it, and is **levered against** it.
Those are the same asset, so "quote asset" and "collateral" name one thing from two sides.

A launchpad factory is bound to one at construction, so **a creator picks their quote asset by
picking the launchpad** — and it is immutable afterwards, for the same reason the 2x toggle is.

```solidity
launch.quote();       // the ERC-20 it trades against
launch.quoteScale();  // 10 ** (18 - decimals): 1 for WETH, 1e10 for cbBTC
factory.minRaise();   // a tenth of a whole token, in that token's units
```

### Native ETH does not reach `Launch`

`Launch` takes its quote as a plain ERC-20 and nothing else. That is what made the quote pluggable
at all: `msg.value`, `call{value:}` and `weth.deposit` have no equivalent for cbBTC, which has no
native form. Buys are `transferFrom`, sells and refunds are `transfer`.

Somebody holding native ETH uses **`QuoteZap`**, which wraps at the edge and forwards in one
transaction — including refunding an overshoot as native ETH rather than as a WETH balance they
never asked for. It holds nothing between calls and has no privileged role. It works only on
WETH-quoted launches; a cbBTC-quoted launch is traded with cbBTC directly, because there is nothing
to wrap.

<Warning>
**Decimals are load-bearing.** cbBTC is 8 decimals and the oracle prices one WHOLE token, so a raw
balance multiplied by a price without scaling under-reports by 1e10. Everything internal stays in
the quote's own units; only the USD boundary scales. Three separate places had to learn this — the
launch (`quoteScale`), the registry (`Collateral.scale`) and the oracle swap router
(`collateralScale`) — and each was caught by testing against a genuinely 8-decimal token rather than
an 18-decimal stand-in.

A fourth was subtler: the curve's spot price in quote-per-token rounds to **zero** for an 8-decimal
quote, so `priceUsd` converts in one step instead of pricing in quote units first.
</Warning>

## The registry

```solidity
struct Collateral {
    IPriceOracle oracle;   // this asset's feed
    ISwapRouter  router;   // this asset's venue for cash conversions
    uint256 pooled;        // sum of poolEth across pools on this asset
    uint256 idle;          // peeled in kind, not yet handed over
    uint256 seniorUsd;     // the claim deployed against this asset
    uint16  capBps;        // share of liability it may back
    bool    enabled;
    uint256 scale;         // 10 ** (18 - decimals): 1 for WETH, 1e10 for cbBTC
}
```

```solidity
earnPool.addCollateral(token, oracle, router, capBps);   // list
earnPool.setCollateralCap(token, capBps);                // retune
earnPool.setCollateralEnabled(token, false);             // wind down
```

`registerPool` reads the asset off the launch itself rather than taking it on trust, so a pool can
never be filed under an asset it does not hold.

## Per-asset risk

$$
\mathrm{CR}_{\text{asset}} = \frac{\sum_{i \in \text{asset}} \mathrm{TVL}_i}{\sum_{i \in \text{asset}} S_i}
$$

The aggregate is unchanged in meaning — `globalCr` is still total assets over total liability — but
each leg is marked with **its own feed**:

```solidity
earnPool.collateralCr(token);            // this asset's cover
earnPool.totalCollateralUsd();           // every asset, each at its own price
earnPool.collateralHeadroomUsd(token);   // how much more senior it may take
```

<Warning>
Isolation is not diversification. An asset-specific shock stays inside that asset's pools and works
itself out through that asset's [routes](/protocol/routes). A broad selloff stresses every asset at
once, and the senior claim — being aggregate — feels a share of it. Isolation contains a bad
listing; it does not make the book market-neutral.
</Warning>

## Dynamic routing

Deposits are cash and do not pick an asset. What picks an asset is **a pool attaching senior against
it**, so that is where the choice is priced.

### The soft half: rent

$$
r_{\text{asset}}(U) = \underbrace{r(U)}_{\text{book-wide utilisation}} + \underbrace{\sigma(\mathrm{CR}_{\text{asset}})}_{\text{routing surcharge}}
$$

$$
\sigma = \text{segment}\!\left(\mathrm{CR}_{\text{asset}};\ 2.0 \to 1.0,\ 0 \to 30\%\ \text{APR}\right)
$$

Zero while the asset is comfortable, rising to the maximum as it approaches bare cover, clamped flat
past it. The same [piecewise-linear shape](/reference/formula-sheet#piecewise-linear-schedules) as
every other reactive price here. Renting senior against a thin collateral costs more than renting
against a thick one, so flow moves toward cover on its own rather than by decree.

`Launch._accrueFunding` calls `fundingRateWad()` with no arguments, and that signature is unchanged:
the Earn Pool looks at whether the caller is a registered pool and returns that pool's asset-aware
rate. A read from a UI or a keeper is not a pool and gets the book-wide rate. `fundingRateFor(token)`
exposes any asset's rate to anyone.

### The hard half: the cap

`lendIdle` refuses a loan that would push an asset past `capBps × liability`. One listing must not be
able to become the whole book's risk however loud its tape.

Hitting the ceiling does not break a coin: `_tryPair` wraps its attach in `try/catch` and returns
false, so the launch graduates unlevered and retries as the book grows or the asset's share falls.
Raising the cap and calling `tryPair()` again pairs it.

## Exit

An exit pays in the collateral of the pools it peeled, so the holder knows in advance which token
they are receiving. Every pool in `peelFrom` must sit on **one** asset — a mixed list reverts with
`mixed collateral` rather than handing somebody a basket they did not choose. They call again per
asset; `redeemableAssets()` says what is reachable.

The same holds for the price-free `emergencyRedeem`: pro-rata within one asset per call, still a pure
ratio of quantities, still working when the oracle does not.

## A dead feed on one asset

`totalCollateralUsd` **skips** a stale leg rather than reverting. This is load-bearing: iterating a
dead oracle would take `nav()` down with it, and through it every priced exit on every other asset —
one stale feed would trap the whole book. The skipped leg shows up as a lower valuation and
`oracleLive()` reports the gap.

Winding an asset down works the same way. `setCollateralEnabled(token, false)` stops new senior
attaching and leaves everything else working: existing pools still rebalance, peel and redeem.

## What this changed in the research files

`pools.csv` carries `quote_symbol` and `quote_decimals` beside `collateral_token`, because
`pool_eth`, `vault_eth` and `reserve_eth` are in the quote's own units — an 8-decimal pool of
`0.99e8` is one whole cbBTC, not 1e-10 of one.

`book.csv` reports `total_collateral_usd` — a USD sum, because collateral quantities across assets
cannot be added. The per-asset breakdown, which is the only form that means anything once there is
more than one listing, is **`collaterals.csv`**: one row per listed asset per interval, carrying its
price, its own `oracle_live`, pooled and idle balances, its senior claim, its CR, its cap and
headroom, and both halves of its rate. `pools.csv` gains `collateral_token`, without which every
per-pool reading is ambiguous.

Postgres mirrors this in `collateral_samples`, one row per asset per sample.

title: Rebalance routes
description: The protocol posts a price and lets arbitrageurs fill it. It never trades for itself.

When a pool drifts away from $L^\star = 2$, something has to move collateral. The protocol does
**not** do that by calling a venue. It posts a **route** at an oracle-derived price and lets whoever
wants the trade fill it.

<Note>
The moment de-risking is most needed is the moment the venue is worst. A rebalance that calls a DEX
with a `minOut` reverts exactly then. A route cannot: a thin book means it fills slowly, not that
the rebalance fails.
</Note>

## Why a route and not a swap

| | Swap the protocol executes | Route the protocol posts |
|---|---|---|
| Execution risk | Slippage, sandwiching, an unpredictable fill | None. The price is fixed by schedule |
| Thin book | Reverts on `minOut` | Fills slowly, or waits |
| MEV | The protocol is a taker, so it can be sandwiched | The protocol is not a taker |
| Pricing input | Oracle **and** venue depth | Oracle only |
| Cost of de-risking | Vanishes into a fill, unattributable | Booked as `routePnlUsd`, signed, per pool |
| Near the target | Pays the spread | **Earns** the spread |

The last row is the one people miss. Near the inner bound the pool barely needs the flow, so it
prices *in its own favour* and is paid to de-risk. Only under real stress does it subsidise.

## The price

$$
\text{routePrice} = P \cdot f(L)
$$

$f$ is a three-point [piecewise-linear curve](/reference/formula-sheet#piecewise-linear-schedules),
clamped flat outside the outermost point. It depends only on $L$ and the control points, never on
market data — which is precisely why a filler will show up: the fill price is knowable before the
transaction is sent.

### Sell route — the pool gives collateral, takes cash

Opens **before** the mandatory edge. A single cliff at $2.5$ leaves ordinary drift unopposed, and
drift is what actually moves a book away from target in a flat market.

| $L$ | $f$ | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| $< 2.20$ | — | Route shut |
| $2.20$ | $1.0050$ | Sells ETH **above** spot. The pool profits |
| $2.50$ | $1.0000$ | Exactly oracle spot |
| $\ge 3.00$ | $0.9800$ | Discounts ETH. The pool pays for urgency |

### Buy route — the pool gives cash, takes collateral

The mirror image, read as leverage falls. Deliberately optional: protecting the senior claim is the
product, restoring 2x after a rally is a convenience.

| $L$ | $f$ | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| $> 1.80$ | — | Route shut |
| $1.80$ | $0.9950$ | Buys ETH **below** spot. The pool profits |
| $1.50$ | $1.0000$ | Exactly oracle spot |
| $\le 1.20$ | $1.0200$ | Pays up |

## How much is on offer

Selling $d$ ETH at $P f$ retires $d P f$ of senior, so the junior moves by $d P (f - 1)$ and the
target solves in closed form:

$$
\mathrm{TVL} - dP = L^\star\left(J + dP(f-1)\right)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
d = \frac{\mathrm{TVL} - L^\star J}{P\left(L^\star f - (L^\star - 1)\right)}
$$

At $f = 1$ this reduces to $d = (\mathrm{TVL} - 2J)/P$. The denominator cannot go non-positive:
$f$ is clamped to $[0.98,\ 1.005]$, so $2f - 1$ stays in $[0.96,\ 1.01]$.

A fill is additionally capped at **15% of `vaultEth`** per transaction, and the buy route can never
offer more than the Earn Pool's idle cash.

## Reading and filling

```solidity
(uint256 ethAvailable, uint256 priceWad) = launch.sellRouteQuote();
usdg.approve(address(launch), usdgIn);
launch.fillSellRoute(usdgIn, minEthOut);   // pay USDG, receive WETH

(uint256 ethWanted, uint256 buyPrice) = launch.buyRouteQuote();
weth.approve(address(launch), ethIn);
launch.fillBuyRoute(ethIn, minUsdgOut);    // pay WETH, receive USDG
```

Both are permissionless. The people who benefit from a sell fill are Earn Pool holders, and they
should never have to wait on a keeper for their claim to stop being eroded.

## Where the spread lands

Every fill books its profit or subsidy to `routePnlUsd` on that pool, and it lands on the
**junior** — the party renting the leverage. De-risking is paid for by the position being
de-risked, never socialised onto the senior claim.

<Note>
Hylo settles the same P&L against its Earn Pool, because hyUSD is a hard \$1 peg and cannot absorb
anything. LYC's NAV floats, so it *could* — but it shouldn't. The senior claim stays clean in both
directions, which is the entire product.
</Note>

## What happens if nobody fills

The book stays levered. This is inherent to any market-incentivised rebalance, Hylo's included, and
it is why the route opens early and pays a premium there rather than waiting for a cliff. The
backstops behind it are the [leverage surcharge](/protocol/fees) on destabilising trades and
[pool-to-pool netting](/protocol/netting), which needs no outside counterparty at all.

## The one remaining swap

`protect()` still calls the router, and is confined to pools whose junior is already gone
($J = 0$). A route needs somebody to fill it and a dead coin has nobody watching, so the orphan
sweep accepts a swap that may revert. Nothing is blocked when it does: the residual stays in ETH and
the call can be retried, and [holder redemptions](/protocol/redeem) are paid in kind and never
depend on it succeeding.

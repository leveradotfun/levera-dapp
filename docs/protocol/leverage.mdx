## vHFyc — the virtual senior unit

`seniorUsd` is not a loose dollar figure. It is the **supply** of a per-pool virtual unit: one vHFyc
is one dollar of the Earn Pool's claim sitting in that pool. It is minted when senior attaches and
burned when senior leaves, exactly like a token.

$$
J = \mathrm{TVL} - S, \qquad
\mathrm{CR} = \frac{\mathrm{TVL}}{S}, \qquad
L = \frac{\mathrm{TVL}}{J} = \frac{\mathrm{CR}}{\mathrm{CR} - 1}
$$

<Note>
This is the same object as Hylo's **vUSD**: an internal accounting unit, not a tradeable token.
Making it transferable would let someone hold one pool's risk without holding HFyc, which breaks the
single-senior-token premise.
</Note>

What it gains over a bare scalar is **history**:

| Field | What it answers |
|---|---|
| `seniorUsd` | Live supply |
| `seniorMintedUsd` / `seniorBurnedUsd` | Churn. Cumulative mint far above the live supply means capital is being shuttled |
| `seniorHighWaterUsd` | The most senior this pool ever carried |
| `pairingBilledUsd` | The basis the [pairing fee](/protocol/fees#the-pairing-fee) has already been charged on |

Every mint and burn goes through one pair of functions, so the supply, the lifetime totals and the
high-water mark can never disagree. The invariant suite asserts
$S = \text{minted} - \text{burned}$ after every call.

## The two cover ratios

A pool holds collateral in two buckets, and each has its own question.

$$
\texttt{seniorCoverage} = \frac{\texttt{vaultEth}}{S / P}
\qquad
\texttt{reserveCover} = \frac{\texttt{reserveEth}}{\texttt{juniorEth}}
$$

**Senior coverage** — how much of the claim is still backed by collateral the pool actually holds.
It starts at $1.0$ when senior attaches and decays with the pairing fee and route spreads. Above
$1.0$ the vault holds *more* than the claim is worth, and the excess belongs to the junior.

**Reserve cover** — whether the quoted price is reachable. The AMM quotes off `juniorEth` but can
only **pay** from `reserveEth`. Below $1.0$, part of the price is backed by vault ETH a seller
cannot take.

### Why reserve cover moves against you on a rally

$$
\texttt{juniorEth} = \texttt{reserveEth} + \texttt{vaultEth} - S/P
$$

As $P$ rises, $S/P$ shrinks — the claim is worth less collateral — so part of `vaultEth` stops
backing senior and starts belonging to the junior, while still sitting where a seller cannot reach
it. Left alone the pool ends up quoting a healthy price it cannot honour.

`rebalanceToReserve()` is the correction, and it is permissionless:

$$
\Delta E = \max(\texttt{vaultEth} - S/P,\ 0)
$$

Only the excess moves. Senior keeps exactly the collateral its claim is worth, so coverage lands on
$1.0$ and the [sell route](/protocol/routes) still has its full backing to work with. TVL, $J$ and
$L$ are all unchanged — this corrects **which bucket** the collateral sits in, nothing else.

## The band

$$
L^\star = 2.0, \qquad \text{no-action band} = [1.5,\ 2.5]
$$

| State | $L$ | $\mathrm{CR}$ | ETH move from a 2x, all-ETH start |
|---|---|---|---|
| Buy route fully open | $1.20$ | $600\%$ | $+150\%$ |
| Buy route opens | $1.80$ | $225\%$ | $+25\%$ |
| Target | $2.00$ | $200\%$ | $0$ |
| Sell route opens | $2.20$ | $183\%$ | $-9.1\%$ |
| Sell route at spot | $2.50$ | $167\%$ | $-16.7\%$ |
| Sell route fully subsidised | $3.00$ | $150\%$ | $-25\%$ |
| Junior gone | $\infty$ | $100\%$ | $-50\%$ |

<Warning>
Those ETH moves assume the pool is **at** $L^\star$. A pool that has drifted is materially closer to
its trigger than the table implies, and only the live figure is true. Publish
$\texttt{eth\_move\_to\_sell\_route\_pct}$ and $\texttt{eth\_move\_to\_wipe\_pct}$ from current
state, never from the target.
</Warning>

At $3\times$ the sell trigger is $\sim -10\%$ and impairment is $-33\%$ — no room to transact. $2\times$
is the first leverage where "sell into the dump" can exist at all.

## What moves leverage

**Toward the sell edge:** occupancy rent (`S` up, TVL flat), the pairing fee (TVL down, `S` flat),
route subsidies, and junior leaving.

**Toward the buy edge:** junior arriving. Adding junior at fair NAV without pairing more senior
lowers $L$, and that is allowed — do not silently turn a depositor's collateral into someone else's
senior.

**Back toward target**, in order of preference:

1. [Pool-to-pool netting](/protocol/netting) — free, no counterparty, both pools improve.
2. [Rebalance routes](/protocol/routes) — priced, permissionless, opens at $2.20$ / $1.80$.
3. The [leverage surcharge](/protocol/fees) — makes the destabilising trade pay for the rebalance it
   just made necessary.

## The allocation law

When idle cash is empty, `reallocateFrom` lets a coin with more **recent volume** pull senior from a
quieter one, in kind. Occupancy rent follows the attached dollars and volume fees are earned where
people actually trade, so scarce capital walks toward the tape.

Same-wallet round trips do not count as recent volume — a wash bot must not outrank a quieter real
tape. Two-wallet wash can still game the score; that residual is accepted.

This is gated on volume, not leverage, so it can disagree with netting. Both exist.

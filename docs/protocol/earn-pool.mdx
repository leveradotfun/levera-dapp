**LYC** is the token. The **Earn Pool** is the contract — `EarnPool.sol`, the book that holds every
senior dollar and aggregates the yield from every 2x coin attached to it.

Holding LYC *is* being in the Earn Pool. There is no second deposit to make and nothing to stake,
which is why the supply never moves when yield arrives:

$$
\text{nav} = \frac{\text{liability}}{\text{supply}}, \qquad
\Delta\text{liability} > 0,\ \Delta\text{supply} = 0 \ \Rightarrow\ \text{nav} \uparrow
$$

<Note>
Hylo separates hyUSD from eHYUSD because yield accrues only to the staked half. Here there is no
staked half: every holder is in the pool, so the aggregation happens in NAV instead of in a second
token's exchange rate.
</Note>

## Where the yield comes from

Four streams, from every 2x coin in the book at once:

| Stream | Assets arrive? | Shares minted? |
|---|---|---|
| Holder slice of the 1.25% trade fee (50 bps, 2x only) | Yes, at harvest | No |
| Pairing fee (50 bps of newly attached senior) | Yes | No |
| [Occupancy rent](/protocol/occupancy) | No — the split moves | No |
| [Route spread](/protocol/routes) earned near the inner bound | Yes | No |

All four raise `liability` without raising `supply`. That is the only thing that lifts NAV.

## APY is measured, not promised

$$
\text{APY} = \frac{\text{yield over the window}}{\text{average liability}} \cdot \frac{\text{year}}{\text{elapsed}}
$$

```solidity
earnPool.earnPoolApyWad();                  // annualised realised return
earnPool.earnPoolWindow();                  // (yieldUsd, elapsed, baseLiability)
earnPool.earnPoolYieldMixWad();             // (occupancyShare, cashShare)
```

Three decisions in that formula, each of which exists because its absence produces a misleading
number:

**A trailing window, not an instant.** Fee income is lumpy — one busy coin arrives as a single large
harvest, and an instantaneous reading taken just after it would annualise a spike into a promise.
The window rolls forward once a day, permissionlessly, and rolling early is a no-op so it cannot be
checkpointed at a flattering moment.

**Averaged liability.** Using the opening figure overstates the rate whenever deposits arrived
mid-window, because the yield was earned on a book that grew.

**Nothing below an hour.** Too short a sample cannot be annualised honestly, so no figure is
published at all rather than one that looks like a measurement.

<Warning>
`fundingRateWad` is **not** APY and must never be shown as one. It is the [occupancy
rate](/protocol/occupancy) 2x coins pay on attached senior — 2% at rest, 10% at healthy
utilisation, up to 70% with the book fully deployed. Holder return is `earnPoolApyWad`, and in a
fee-driven book the two are not close: rent has been a rounding error next to the fee take.
</Warning>

## Publish the mix, not just the number

`earnPoolYieldMixWad()` splits the return between rent and cash income, and it belongs next to the
headline. A book whose return is 99% trade fees behaves completely differently from one whose return
is 99% rent, and only one of them survives a quiet day. Inferring that from two lifetime counters is
work no reader should have to do.

## What the senior claim is worth

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{liability} &= \textstyle\sum_i S_i + U_{\text{idle}} + E_{\text{idle}} \cdot P \\
\text{globalCR} &= \frac{\sum_i \mathrm{TVL}_i + U_{\text{idle}} + E_{\text{idle}} \cdot P}{\text{liability}}
\end{aligned}
$$

`ethDropToImpairmentWad()` turns that into the number that actually matters to a holder: how far
collateral can fall, ignoring any rebalancing, before the junior across the whole book is gone and
the senior claim starts taking losses. It is the gap-risk figure, not the grind figure — the grind
is what the [routes](/protocol/routes) are for.

Both belong on the page. Derive them from live state, never from the $L^\star = 2$ target: a book
that has drifted to $L = 2.3$ is a materially riskier book than the target implies, and only one of
those two numbers is true at any moment.

## Supply accounting

$$
\text{supply} = \text{depositMinted} + \text{feeMinted}
$$

NAV yield increases neither. If a page shows "price up" from a fee mint, it is lying: a fee mint is
assets in *then* shares out at the prevailing NAV, which is a deposit, not yield.

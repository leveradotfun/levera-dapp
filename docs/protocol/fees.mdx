A 5% APR on a coin that lives two minutes is $\sim 0.00002\%$ of senior. Do not try to make occupancy rent pay for a 2-minute frenzy. Do not try to make volume fees pay for a 12-hour book that nobody trades.

The Earn Pool is paid by **three streams with three jobs**. This page is the trade-fee stream. Pairing is on [Graduation](/mechanics/graduation). Occupancy is on [Occupancy](/protocol/occupancy).

## Total trade fee

$$
f(L) = \underbrace{125\ \text{bps}}_{\text{flat}} + \underbrace{\sigma(L)}_{\text{leverage surcharge}}
$$

Charged on the **quote leg** — ETH for a WETH-quoted coin, cbBTC for a cbBTC-quoted one. The flat part is never raised to feed the Earn
Pool. The surcharge is not income at all — see below.

Buyer pays fee-inclusive; seller receives fee-exclusive.

### Buy (quote in)

$$
\text{fee} = 125\,\text{bps} \cdot \text{quoteIn}, \quad
\text{surcharge} = \sigma \cdot \text{quoteIn}, \quad
\text{net} = \text{quoteIn} - \text{fee} - \text{surcharge}
$$

### Sell (quote out)

$$
\text{quoteOut} = \text{grossQuote} - \text{fee} - \text{surcharge}
$$

On the curve, the contract solves for the **gross** (in quote units) that, after fee, equals the constant-product
output, so the seller still sees $1.25\%$ off the top.

## The leverage surcharge

The trade that drags the book toward an edge is the one that forces the protocol to act, so it is
the one that should pay for it. A flat fee makes that externality free and charges it to everybody
else.

$$
\sigma(L) = \text{juniorFeeBps}(L) - 50\ \text{bps}
$$

`juniorFeeBps` is a [piecewise-linear curve](/reference/formula-sheet#piecewise-linear-schedules)
with two control points per direction — base at the target, maximum at the edge the trade is
pushing into:

| Trade | At $L^\star = 2$ | At the edge it pushes toward | Ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|
| Buy (junior arrives, $L$ falls) | 50 bps | 300 bps at $L = 1.5$ | 700 bps |
| Sell (junior leaves, $L$ rises) | 50 bps | 300 bps at $L = 2.5$ | 700 bps |

Each direction is **free in the direction that helps**: arriving junior rebuilds the cushion, so it
is never surcharged when the book is over-levered, and vice versa. With the junior wiped, leaving is
at the ceiling and arriving is at the floor — new capital is the only thing that can rebuild a
cushion and must not be taxed like an exit.

<Note>
This is Hylo's anti-destabilisation fee schedule, as a continuous curve rather than a discrete
zone table. It is a backstop, not a routine cost: in normal operation the
[routes](/protocol/routes) and [netting](/protocol/netting) hold the book near target and this
barely binds.
</Note>

### The cover surcharge

Exits carry one more term, zero while the Earn Pool is comfortably covered and rising to 400 bps at
full impairment:

$$
\text{crSurcharge} = \text{segment}\!\left(\mathrm{CR}_{\text{global}},\ 1.5 \to 1.0,\ 0 \to 400\ \text{bps}\right)
$$

It lands on **exits only**. Arriving junior brings assets in with no matching liability, so it lifts
cover — taxing that would discourage the one trade that helps. This is what lets senior redemption
stay open in every state instead of being gated.

## The surcharge is cushion, not revenue

The surcharge is **not** split with the creator, the protocol or the Earn Pool. It goes straight
into the AMM reserve via `MemePair.donateEth` and stays with the people still holding the position:

$$
\texttt{reserveEth} \mathrel{+}= \text{surcharge}, \qquad \texttt{poolEth} \mathrel{+}= \text{surcharge}
$$

On a 2x pair this raises `juniorEth` and therefore the quote, which is the correct reading of
"someone gave the pool collateral and asked for nothing". The lifetime total is `cushionEthPaid`.

## Split of the flat 125 bps

| Slice | 2x bps | 1x bps | Destination |
|---|---|---|---|
| Creator | 30 | 30 | The coin's **quote asset** (claimable — cbBTC on a cbBTC coin, WETH on a WETH one), or LYC mint-at-NAV **only if 2x and the creator opted in** |
| Protocol | 45 | 95 | LYC mint-at-NAV to the treasury (backed; NAV unchanged) |
| Earn Pool holders | 50 | **0** | USDG in, **no mint**, NAV up. **2x only.** |

On-chain this is `Launch._accrueFeeEth`:

- Creator always takes $30/125$ of the fee (in quote units — the amounts are denominated in the coin's quote asset whatever it is).
- If `!leverageEnabled`, the rest ($95$ bps of notional) is protocol. Return. No holder slice.

<Note>
**A 1x creator cannot opt into LYC.** `LaunchpadFactory.createLaunch` requires
`leverageEnabled || !creatorFeeInHfyc` ("1x quote fees"): a 1x coin never pairs against the Earn
Pool, so paying its creator in LYC would mint them senior out of trade fees with no occupancy and
no pairing behind it. A 2x creator chooses freely — the quote asset or LYC — and the choice is
frozen at creation.
</Note>

## The pairing fee

$$
\text{pairingFee} = 50\ \text{bps} \times \left(S_{\text{after}} - \texttt{pairingBilledUsd}\right)
$$

Billed against the **basis already charged**, not against each attach. Three attaches summing to
$S$ pay exactly the same 50 bps as one attach of $S$ — and, more importantly, senior that leaves on
a route fill or a redemption peel and then comes back is **not charged twice for the same dollars**.

<Warning>
Charging per attach would make every detach/reattach cycle a fresh 50 bps out of the junior. Both
sides of that cycle are permissionless, so it would be a way to mint Earn Pool yield by shuttling
capital between two pools rather than by doing anything useful. The invariant suite asserts
`pairingBilledUsd <= seniorHighWaterUsd` after every call.
</Warning>

The fee is paid from `vaultEth`, converted to USDG and deposited as unminted yield, so attaching
senior slightly raises that pool's leverage.

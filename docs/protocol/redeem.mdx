title: Redeem

A holder is never locked in.

Exit pays whatever the book actually holds — idle cash first, then collateral peeled from the
quietest 2x pools — and it does **not** convert anything on the holder's behalf. There is no swap
anywhere in the path and therefore no liquidity condition on it. If the book holds the assets, the
holder gets them.

<Warning>
The moment a redemption has to route through a venue, a holder's access to their own money depends
on a fill they never asked for, at a size they did not choose. A bad book then leaves them with
neither the cash nor the collateral. That is a lock, and no amount of client-side chunking fixes it.
</Warning>

## The three paths

```solidity
earnPool.redeem(shares);                      // cash only; reverts if idle is short
earnPool.redeemInKind(shares, peelFrom);      // cash + WETH; cannot fail on liquidity
earnPool.emergencyRedeem(shares, peelFrom);   // pro-rata, needs no price at all
```

| Path | When | Pays | Can it fail on liquidity? |
|---|---|---|---|
| `redeem` | Idle cash covers it | USDG | It says `idle cash short: use redeemInKind` |
| `redeemInKind` | Always | USDG, then WETH | No |
| `emergencyRedeem` | Oracle dead **or** $\mathrm{CR}_{\mathrm{global}} \le 1$ | Pro-rata USDG + WETH | No |

`peelFrom` must be registered pools in **ascending** recent volume. That is the allocation law:
scarce senior leaves the quiet tape before the loud one. An unsorted list is rejected.

## In kind, in one call

$$
\begin{aligned}
U_{\text{out}} &= \min(U_{\text{idle}},\ \text{shares} \cdot \text{nav}) \\
E_{\text{out}} &= \frac{\text{shortfall}}{P} \quad\text{peeled quietest-first}
\end{aligned}
$$

Each pool hands over WETH and retires the matching claim in the same step:

$$
\texttt{vaultEth} \mathrel{-}= E, \qquad S_i \mathrel{-}= E \cdot P
$$

Both legs are the same size at the oracle price, so $J = \mathrm{TVL} - S$ is **unchanged**. The
junior neither gains nor loses from somebody else's exit — what the meme loses is leverage, not
value.

There is **no per-call cap** on this path. The 15% cap exists to stop one transaction dumping a
vault into a thin book; an in-kind transfer touches no book at all. It is also strictly cheaper: the
old swap path gave up to 1% away to the router on every exit, and in kind that stays with the people
involved.

<Note>
One call settles everything the book can reach. A sliver can stay behind because the pairing fee
already spent part of the vault, so the claim is worth marginally more collateral than the vault
still holds. The remainder is backed by the pool but only through its AMM reserve — and taking that
would mean dumping somebody's memecoin to pay a senior exit. It tops back up from fees and route
fills instead.
</Note>

## Then, and only then, a conversion

| Holder wants | Book holds | What happens | Who bears execution risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| USDG | Enough idle | Paid from idle | Nobody |
| ETH | Vault ETH | Peeled in kind as WETH | Nobody |
| Basket | Anything | Pro-rata USDG + WETH | Nobody |
| USDG | Only vault ETH | Receives WETH, **then** swaps it themselves | The holder, by choice |

That last row is the only place a swap remains, and it belongs to the person who asked for the
conversion — their transaction, their slippage tolerance. A failed conversion costs a retry instead
of trapping the withdrawal. The app can still offer it as a one-click zap *after* the redeem settles.

## The price-free path

Every figure in `emergencyRedeem` is a ratio of quantities — this holder's shares over the supply,
applied to each asset — so it works when the oracle does not.

That matters. `nav()` fails closed on a stale or wide feed, which is correct for minting and for
sizing a rebalance and **wrong for exit**. A holder must never be unable to leave because a feed went
quiet. It is also the impaired-book path: below full cover, paying the first wallet \$1 would be
taking it from the last, so everyone prices the same way.

It is refused while the book is covered and the feed is live, because pro-rata deliberately claims no
share of the junior cushion — using it on a healthy book would short-change the holder.

Shares burned scale with what was actually delivered. Burning a full position against a smaller
payout would quietly hand the difference to whoever stayed.

## Redeem fee

$$
\text{fee} = 10\ \text{bps of shares},\ \text{left unminted}
$$

The exiting holder benefited from the rebalancing that kept the book solvent, and the remaining
holders inherit the cost, so the exit is what should fund it.

## What a peel does to the meme

It lowers that coin's leverage and its occupancy base. It does **not** touch `reserveEth`, so it does
not dump the AMM — meme holders feel a smaller 2x overlay, not a sack. See
[reserve cover](/protocol/leverage#reserve-cover) for the bucket the reserve belongs to.

## Reentrancy

`receiveSeniorInKind` and `moveSeniorToIdle` are deliberately **not** `nonReentrant`. `_redeem`
already holds the Earn Pool's lock when a pool calls back into them; guarding them too made every
exit that needed a peel revert `ReentrancyGuardReentrantCall` (`0x3ee5aeb5`). `onlyPool` is the
access control, and the pool is itself `nonReentrant` on the release side.

Before paying, `_settleOccupancy` walks up to 32 pools from `occupancyCursor`, so pending rent is
on-chain before any share is burned.

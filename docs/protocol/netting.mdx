---
title: Pool-to-pool netting
description: One book's spare senior answering another's shortfall directly. No ETH sold, no cash spent, no counterparty.
icon: arrow-left-right
---

The cheapest rebalance in the system, and the one to try before opening a
[route](/protocol/routes).

A pool above target is carrying senior it does not need. A pool below target wants exactly that.
Matching them directly moves the claim and the collateral backing it from one book to the other, and
**both pools move toward $L^\star = 2$ in the same transaction**.

```solidity
uint256 usdMoved = earnPool.netSeniorBetween(overLeveredPool, underLeveredPool);
```

Permissionless, and self-sizing: it moves the smaller of what the source has spare and what the
destination is short, so it cannot overshoot on either side.

## What it costs

Nothing. No collateral is sold, no cash is spent, no spread is paid, and no outside counterparty has
to show up:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{source:} \quad &\texttt{vaultEth} \mathrel{-}= \Delta E, \quad S_{\text{src}} \mathrel{-}= \Delta E \cdot P \\
\text{dest:} \quad &\texttt{vaultEth} \mathrel{+}= \Delta E, \quad S_{\text{dst}} \mathrel{+}= \Delta E \cdot P
\end{aligned}
$$

Compare that with the alternative it replaces: the source opens a sell route (paying or earning a
spread, waiting for a filler), the cash lands in idle, and the destination opens a buy route (a
second spread, a second filler). Two spreads and two counterparties, to end in the same place.

## Why nobody is worse off

**The Earn Pool is untouched by construction.** Its total claim is identical before and after — only
the distribution across pools changes. `liability` does not move.

**Neither junior gains or loses.** Collateral and claim move together at the oracle price, so
$J = \mathrm{TVL} - S$ is unchanged on both sides:

$$
\Delta J = -\Delta E \cdot P + \Delta E \cdot P = 0
$$

What each side receives is the leverage it was asking for. The source's $L$ falls because its TVL
shrank against an unchanged junior; the destination's rises for the mirror reason.

## The guards

```
require(source.seniorExcessUsd() > 0)      // the source genuinely has spare
require(dest.seniorGapUsd()    > 0)        // the destination genuinely is short
```

Either one missing would turn this into a way to strip a healthy coin's leverage on demand. The
source-side guard is re-checked inside `releaseToPool`, so the pool refuses even if the entry point
is bypassed.

Occupancy is settled on both pools first, because rent moves the senior/junior split and therefore
moves the very numbers the size is computed from.

## The sizing quantities

$$
\begin{aligned}
\texttt{seniorExcessUsd} &= \max(S - J,\ 0) \quad&&\text{what a pool can give} \\
\texttt{seniorGapUsd} &= \max(J - S,\ 0) \quad&&\text{what a pool wants}
\end{aligned}
$$

Both are zero at exactly $L = 2$, where $S = J$. Note that at $L^\star = 2$ these are the same
quantity as $|\mathrm{TVL} - 2J|$, which is why netting and the routes agree about how far a pool is
from target.

## It works when nothing else does

No venue is touched, so venue depth is irrelevant. A book that cannot swap, cannot fill a route, and
cannot find an arbitrageur can still net two of its own pools against each other.

<Note>
This is the same move Hylo makes when it burns vUSD on one pool and mints it on another during a
rebalance: collateral moves between pools while the aggregate virtual-stablecoin supply stays in
balance. The difference is that Hylo routes it through a USDC pool and a priced swap, and here the
two pools are matched directly.
</Note>

## Reallocation is not netting

[`reallocateFrom`](/protocol/leverage#the-allocation-law) also moves senior between pools in kind,
but it is gated on **recent volume**, not on leverage: scarce capital walking toward the tape. The
two exist for different reasons and can disagree — a loud coin may pull senior from a quiet one that
was already at target. Netting only ever fires when both sides want the move.
